## Full description (max 4000 chars)

```
ScrollLess shows you the truth about short-video scrolling: how many Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts you actually watch, and how long you spend doing it — every day, per app, with a live counter floating right over the feed.

HOW IT WORKS
• Automatic counting. ScrollLess notices each time a new Reel or Short appears on screen and counts it once. Flicking past videos, scrolling back to one you already saw, or expanding a caption doesn't inflate the number.
• Time, per app. See how long the Reels or Shorts viewer was open and how long you were in Instagram or YouTube at all — tracked separately for each app.
• Live floating counter. A small, draggable pill shows today's count and time while you're in Instagram or YouTube. Tap it to open ScrollLess, or turn it off in Settings.
• History. A 7-day and 30-day view with daily totals, averages per active day and a day-by-day breakdown.
• Recent detections. Every count is listed with the app, time and detection confidence, so you can see exactly what was counted.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
• Everything is stored on your phone in a local database. There is no account, no sync, no analytics and no ads.
• ScrollLess reads only the Instagram and YouTube screens. Other apps are ignored.
• What you watch is never stored or sent anywhere — each detection keeps only a short hash of the on-screen text.
• The only data that leaves your device is an anonymous crash report if the app crashes.
• Delete today's data or everything with one tap.

ABOUT THE ACCESSIBILITY SERVICE
ScrollLess uses Android's Accessibility API as its core function: it is the only way for an app to notice that a new short video has appeared in another app. You turn the service on yourself in Android Settings → Accessibility, and you can turn it off at any time. The service does not perform actions for you, does not take screenshots and does not record what you type.

GOOD TO KNOW
• Works with the official Instagram and YouTube apps on Android 8.0 and newer.
• Counting relies on how Instagram and YouTube build their screens; after a major update of those apps, a ScrollLess update may be needed. Detections carry a "high" or "medium" confidence label so you can tell which path was used.
• Some phones (for example with aggressive battery optimisation) may switch accessibility services off after a restart. If counting stops, re-enable ScrollLess in Accessibility settings.
• ScrollLess is open source: github.com/ChandraChaan/scroll-less

ScrollLess is not affiliated with Instagram, Meta, YouTube or Google. Instagram and YouTube are trademarks of their respective owners.
```
